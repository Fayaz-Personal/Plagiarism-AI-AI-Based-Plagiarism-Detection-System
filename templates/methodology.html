System Processing Pipeline

Multi-stage document ingestion and mathematical comparison workflow

1. Document Ingestion (PDF / Word .docx / Plain Text .txt)
   • SHA-256 cryptographic digest calculation for duplicate prevention
   • Format parsing and text stream extraction
2. Unicode NFKC Normalization & Tokenization
   • Standardization of curved quotes, dashes, hyphens, and whitespace
3. Sentence Segmentation with Abbreviation Masking
   • Boundary detection protecting academic abbreviations (e.g., i.e., Dr., prof., al.)
4. Vector Space Representation (TF-IDF)
   • Sublinear term weighting across shared comparison corpus (unigrams + bigrams)
5. High-Dimensional Cosine Similarity Matrix Evaluation
   • Angle calculation invariant to document length
6. Granular Sentence-Level Alignment (≥ 0.70)
   • SequenceMatcher token diffing for word-level overlap highlighting
7. Academic Aggregation & PDF Report Compilation

Mathematical Formulations

Algorithmic metrics utilized in similarity scoring

1. TF-IDF (Term Frequency – Inverse Document Frequency)

TF-IDF evaluates the relative information value of vocabulary words in a submitted paper compared to the reference corpus. Informative technical terms receive higher weights, while common stop words are discounted.

TF-IDF(t, d, D) = (1 + ln(count(t, d))) × [ln((1 + |D|) / (1 + df(t))) + 1]

2. Cosine Similarity in Euclidean Vector Space

Measures the cosine of the angle between two sparse TF-IDF vectors. Because it computes vector angles rather than Euclidean distances, score accuracy is independent of document length.

Cosine Similarity(→u, →v) = (→u • →v) / (||→u|| × ||→v||) = (∑ uk vk) / [sqrt(∑ uk2) × sqrt(∑ vk2)]

3. Deduplicated Text Coverage

Calculates the proportion of the submitted paper composed of flagged sentences, deduplicated across reference documents.

Coverage (%) = [∑ Length(s) for s ∈ UniqueMatchedSentences / ∑ Length(s) for s ∈ AllSubmittedSentences] × 100

Scholarly Standards & Reviewer Obligations

Similarity scores reflect textual overlap, not authorial intent or academic dishonesty. An elevated score does not automatically confirm plagiarism. Academic evaluators must inspect flagged passages in context to identify:

  • Proper Attribution: Direct quotations enclosed in quotation marks with scholarly citations.
  • Technical Terminology: Fixed mathematical or scientific phrases that cannot be meaningfully altered.
  • Methodology Boilerplate: Common descriptions of standard laboratory instruments or computational tools.
  • Bibliographic References: Formal reference entries listed according to APA, IEEE, or ACM standards.
Institutional Mandate: Computational similarity analysis provides supporting data for academic review. Final determinations regarding original scholarship remain the responsibility of qualified human instructors.
